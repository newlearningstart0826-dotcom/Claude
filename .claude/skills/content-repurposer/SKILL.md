# Content Repurposer

A systematic skill that extracts the intellectual capital locked inside a book and rebuilds
it as platform-native content — LinkedIn posts, Twitter/X threads, YouTube scripts,
newsletter series, podcast outlines, and course frameworks — each one designed to build
the author's authority, grow their audience, and generate leads and revenue long after
launch day.

**Why this is the money skill:**
A book is written once. Its ideas can generate content for years. Most authors finish
their book and exhaust themselves on a 30-day launch sprint, then go quiet. The authors
who build businesses from books treat the manuscript as a content mine — a body of
thinking they've already done the hard work to develop, now ready to be reshaped for
the platforms where their audience actually lives.

One book, systematically repurposed, can produce:
- 50–100 LinkedIn posts (12–18 months of weekly content)
- 20–30 Twitter/X threads
- 10–15 YouTube scripts or podcast episodes
- A 12-part newsletter series
- A full online course framework
- A keynote speech structure

This skill builds all of it.

## The Content Mining Framework

Before generating content for any platform, extract the **Content Assets** from the book.
These are the raw units that get reshaped for each channel.

### Step 1 — Content Asset Extraction

Mine the book for these asset types:

**Ideas** — Standalone concepts, frameworks, or principles that can be explained in isolation.
Each key idea from each chapter is a potential content asset.

**Arguments** — Claims that challenge conventional wisdom, reframe a problem, or make a
provocative assertion. These become the best-performing LinkedIn posts and Twitter threads.

**Stories** — Anecdotes, case studies, and examples from the book. The most shareable content
type. Every story in the book is a content asset.

**Frameworks** — The author's named models, processes, or systems. These become carousels,
diagrams, thread structures, and course modules.

**Contrasts** — "Most people do X. The best do Y." moments. High-engagement format on every
platform.

**Statistics & Research** — Data points and study findings that support the book's claims.
Credibility builders and hook material.

**Quotes & Principles** — Short, memorable distillations of the book's ideas. Highly
shareable, good for visual content.

**Questions** — The questions the book raises and answers. Each major question is a content
prompt: pose it to the audience before answering it.

## Module 1 — LinkedIn Content Engine

LinkedIn is where most business non-fiction authors have their highest-ROI content
presence. The platform rewards ideas-driven long-form posts, personal stories tied to
professional insights, and frameworks that make readers say "I'm saving this."

### LinkedIn Content Types (matched to book asset types)

| Content Type | Best Asset | Typical Performance Driver |
|---|---|---|
| **The Insight Post** | A single idea, fully unpacked | Saves/shares from people who want to remember it |
| **The Story Post** | A case study or personal anecdote → lesson | Comments from people who recognise themselves |
| **The Contrarian Post** | An argument against conventional wisdom | Debate in comments = reach |
| **The Framework Post** | A named model or process | Saves; often shared in teams |
| **The List Post** | 5–7 principles, lessons, or observations | Shares from people tagging colleagues |
| **The Question Post** | A question the audience has never been asked directly | Comments from people who want to engage |
| **The Before/After Post** | Transformation story — what changed and why | Resonance with people in the "before" state |
| **The Thread Summary** | "I wrote a book. Here's what I learned." | High-volume reach; book awareness driver |

### LinkedIn Post Structure

Every high-performing LinkedIn post has the same skeleton:

```
LINE 1 (The Hook):   One sentence. Creates a question, contradiction, or strong claim.
                     Must stand alone. The reader decides to click "see more" based on
                     this line only.

LINE 2 (The Gap):    A white space. Intentional. Forces the hook line to breathe.

LINES 3–8 (The Body): The idea developed. Short paragraphs, 1–3 sentences each.
                      Alternates between claim and evidence, or story and lesson.
                      Each paragraph earns the next.

LAST LINE (The Close): Either a call-to-action question (to drive comments) or a
                       sharp distillation of the whole post in one sentence. Never
                       "What do you think?" — too weak. Ask something specific.
```

### LinkedIn Content Calendar Output

Generate a **full LinkedIn content bank** — one post per asset, ready to publish.

Structure: For each post, produce the full post text + a metadata block.

```
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Recommended timing: [Week N of content calendar]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

[Full post text — publication-ready]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

AUDIENCE SIGNAL:   [Who will self-identify with this post — use as targeting signal]
BOOK TIE:          [How naturally to mention the book if at all — hard sell / soft mention / none]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

Generate a minimum of **12 posts** (3 months of weekly content) per run. Offer to extend
to 50+ for a full year's bank.

## Module 2 — Twitter / X Thread Engine

Twitter/X rewards different content than LinkedIn: faster pace, sharper claims, more
raw and immediate voice. Threads are the primary long-form format — a sequence of tweets
that develop a single idea from hook to conclusion.

### Thread Types (matched to book assets)

| Thread Type | Best Asset | What makes it spread |
|---|---|---|
| **The Argument Thread** | A contrarian claim with supporting points | Retweets from people who agree; quote-tweets from people who don't |
| **The Story Thread** | A case study or narrative with a lesson at each stage | Completion — readers stay for the resolution |
| **The Framework Thread** | A named model broken into numbered steps | Saves; referenced in other threads |
| **The Lessons Thread** | "I spent X years doing Y. Here's what I learned." | Relatability; reach from people in the same field |
| **The Myth-Busting Thread** | 5–7 common beliefs in the field, each dismantled | Shares from people who feel vindicated |
| **The Research Thread** | A surprising finding with context and implications | Credibility; shared by curious readers |
| **The How-To Thread** | A step-by-step process from the book | Saves; direct utility |
| **The Hot Take Thread** | One strong opinion with the defence built in | Engagement through agreement and disagreement |

### Thread Structure

```
TWEET 1 (Hook):       The entire thread's value proposition in one tweet.
                      Formula: "[Surprising claim / Intriguing question / Bold number]"
                      Ends with: "Here's what [I learned / the data shows / most people miss]:"
                      (the colon signals there's more coming)

TWEETS 2–N (Body):    Each tweet = one point, fully made.
                      Number them: "2/" or "2/10"
                      Short sentences. One idea per tweet. End with a micro-hook
                      that makes the reader want the next tweet.

FINAL TWEET (CTA):    The payoff tweet. Either:
                      - A synthesis (the whole argument in one sentence)
                      - A question (that makes people reply)
                      - A soft book mention ("This is from Chapter N of [Book] — link in bio")
```

### Thread Output Format

```
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TWITTER/X THREAD [N of N]
Source: Ch [N] — [Asset type]
Thread length: [N tweets]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TWEET 1/ [Hook tweet — full text]

TWEET 2/ [Body tweet — full text]

TWEET 3/ [Body tweet — full text]

[Continue...]

TWEET [N]/ [Final tweet — synthesis + optional book mention]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SPREAD MECHANISM: [What will make this thread get shared]
BEST TIME TO POST: [Category: Monday AM / midweek / Sunday night — rationale]
REPLY PROMPT:      [The question to pin as first reply to drive engagement]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

Generate a minimum of **10 threads** per run — a 10-week thread bank.

## Module 3 — YouTube / Video Script Engine

YouTube rewards educational depth. Unlike LinkedIn and Twitter, it's a *search* platform —
people find videos by searching for problems, not by following feeds. This changes what
content to make: title-first thinking, where the SEO keyword is the premise.

### Video Content Types

| Format | Length | Best Asset | Revenue model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Explainer** | 8–12 min | A single framework or concept | Views → authority → book/course sales |
| **The Argument Video** | 6–10 min | A contrarian claim with evidence | Watch time + comments → channel growth |
| **The Tutorial** | 10–20 min | A step-by-step process | Direct utility → subscribers |
| **The Case Study** | 8–15 min | A story with a clear before/after | Identification → leads |
| **The Myth-Buster** | 8–12 min | 3–5 false beliefs dismantled | Search traffic on common misconceptions |
| **The Chapter Summary** | 5–8 min | One chapter, distilled | Book awareness; long-tail search |
| **The Deep Dive** | 20–40 min | A full chapter or major argument | Authority; high-intent audience |

### YouTube Script Structure

```
[HOOK — 0:00–0:45]
The most important 45 seconds in the video. Three beats:
  Beat 1: The problem or question (why the viewer should care)
  Beat 2: The payoff (what they'll know by the end)
  Beat 3: A credibility signal (why this author is worth 10 minutes)
No intro. No "welcome back." Start with the viewer's problem.

[CONTEXT — 0:45–2:00]
Why this matters. What the conventional approach gets wrong. Stakes.

[CORE CONTENT — 2:00–[END-2:00]]
The idea developed in full. Section by section.
Each section: claim → explanation → example/story → implication.
Visual suggestion at each section: [what to show on screen]

[CLOSE — last 2 min]
Synthesis: the one thing to remember.
CTA: specific (not "like and subscribe" — "if you want to go deeper, [X]")
Book mention: natural, not promotional.
```

## Module 4 — Newsletter Series Engine

A newsletter series turns the book into an ongoing relationship with a subscriber list —
delivering value over weeks or months, with each issue driving deeper into the book's ideas
and building the author's brand as a trusted expert.

### Newsletter Series Types

**The Book Breakdown Series** — One chapter per issue. Readers who haven't bought the
book get the ideas; readers who have get added depth. 10–15 issue series.

**The Theme Series** — One big idea from the book explored across 4–6 issues from
different angles. Multiple series possible — one per major theme in the book.

**The Application Series** — "How to apply [book idea] to [specific context]." Practical,
action-oriented, directly tied to reader outcomes.

**The Behind-the-Book Series** — The stories, research, and thinking that didn't make it
into the book. Rewards subscribers with exclusive depth.

**The Ongoing Column** — The book as a lens. Every issue applies the book's framework to
something happening in the world, in business, or in the reader's life. Can run indefinitely.

### Newsletter Issue Structure

```
SUBJECT LINE:     [One of 3 options — curiosity / utility / provocation]
PREVIEW TEXT:     [The sentence that appears after the subject in inbox — 40–60 chars]

OPENING (50–100 words):
  Either: A short story that creates the issue's question
  Or: A direct statement of what this issue delivers and why it matters today

BODY (400–700 words):
  The idea developed in depth. More nuanced than social posts.
  Can include: a framework, a story, research, a case study, a personal observation.
  Ends each section with a practical implication for the reader.

THE TAKEAWAY (50–100 words):
  One concrete thing the reader can think, do, or notice differently this week.
  The "minimum viable action" from this issue's idea.

THE LINK (optional):
  A natural mention of the book, a relevant resource, or a relevant offer.
  Never the first thing. Always earns its place.

SIGN-OFF:
  Consistent, personal. Sounds like the author.
```

### Newsletter Series Output

```
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

[Full issue text — publication-ready]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ISSUE 2 of [N]
[Same structure]

[Continue for full series]

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
SERIES ARCHITECTURE NOTE:
  [How the issues build on each other — the arc of the series as a whole]
  [What the subscriber knows / can do / believes differently by the final issue]
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

Generate a minimum of a **12-issue series** (one quarter of weekly content) per run.

## Module 6 — Course & Paid Product Framework

The highest-revenue repurposing path. Turns the book into a structured learning product —
a course, workshop, or cohort programme — where readers pay for transformation, not
just information.

### The Book-to-Course Logic

A book delivers ideas. A course delivers *practice* — the structured application of those
ideas until the reader can execute them. Readers who want to implement, not just understand,
will pay for the course version.

**Pricing signal:** A book at £15–£25 has a natural audience ceiling. A course at £200–
£2,000 has a different audience — smaller, more committed, higher intent. The book is the
marketing for the course.

### Course Framework Structure

```
COURSE FRAMEWORK: "[Course Title]"
Based on: [Book title]
Target student: [Who this is for — more specific than the book's reader]
Outcome promise: "[By the end, students will be able to: X]"
Delivery format: [Self-paced / Cohort / Live workshop / Hybrid]
Suggested price point: [£/$ range + rationale]
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

MODULE STRUCTURE:

MODULE 1: "[Title]" (from Ch [N])
  Learning objective: [What students can do after this module]
  Lessons:
    1.1 [Lesson title — the concept explained]
    1.2 [Lesson title — the evidence and examples]
    1.3 [Lesson title — the application]
  Exercise: [The specific practice activity that builds the skill]
  Deliverable: [What students produce — something they keep and use]

MODULE 2: "[Title]" (from Ch [N])
  [Same structure]

[Continue for all modules]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COURSE ARCHITECTURE NOTES:
  What the book does that the course doesn't need to: [Concepts to cut]
  What the course does that the book can't: [Exercises, accountability, community]
  Upsell / continuation product: [What comes after this course]
```

## Repurposing Principles

**Platform-native, not platform-adapted.** A LinkedIn post is not a chapter summary.
A YouTube script is not a blog post read to camera. Each platform has a native format
that performs — use it, don't fight it.

**Ideas travel further than promotion.** The content that builds the author's audience
and business is not "buy my book" content. It's the ideas from the book, given away
freely — with the book as the place to go deeper. Generosity is the best marketing.

**One idea, many expressions.** The same concept from Chapter 3 can become a LinkedIn
insight post, a Twitter thread, a YouTube explainer, and a newsletter deep-dive — each
reaching a different segment of the audience in their preferred format.

**Recency beats archive.** A book published 3 years ago still contains ideas worth
sharing today. The content engine doesn't stop when the launch does — it compounds.

**Content signals product-market fit.** The posts that get saved, the threads that get
shared, the newsletter issues that get replied to — these tell the author which ideas
resonate most. Use them to decide what to build next: a course, a workshop, a second book.

## Integration with Other Skills

**← voice-calibrator**: Every piece of content is written in the author's Voice
Fingerprint. Paste it before generating any module. Platform-native doesn't mean
voice-abandoned.

**← book-architect + chapter-builder**: Chapter key ideas, frameworks, stories, and
arguments are the direct input to the Content Asset Inventory. The richer the book
architecture, the richer the content mine.

**← book-launch-kit**: The launch kit handles the 30-day launch window. This skill
handles the 12–24 months that follow. They don't overlap; they sequence. Run
book-launch-kit first, then content-repurposer for the long-game plan.

**← narrative-spine-builder**: The Central Metaphor and Spine Story from the narrative
spine are the most powerful recurring content elements — the through-line that makes
the author's content feel like a coherent body of thought, not disconnected posts.
