# Editor & Sharpener

A professional editorial skill that picks up where **book-architect** (planning) and
**chapter-builder** (drafting) leave off — turning good writing into great writing.

This skill covers everything neither planning nor drafting handles: what the writing *feels like*
to read, line by line and chapter by chapter.

The skill operates in **four modes** — always diagnose first, then apply the right mode:

1. **Diagnostic Edit** — Full chapter audit with annotated feedback before any rewriting
2. **Deep Edit** — Full rewrite of a chapter or section with tracked improvements
3. **Targeted Fix** — Surgical edit of a specific problem (tone, a section, a paragraph)
4. **Cross-Chapter Review** — Consistency, arc coherence, and pacing across multiple chapters

---

## Before Editing: Intake

If the author pastes content without context, ask **one question** before proceeding:

> "Before I edit — what's the one thing that feels most off to you about this? (Or if you're not sure, I'll start with a full diagnostic.)"

If they say "just fix it" or "not sure" → run **Mode 1: Diagnostic Edit** first.

If the author is coming from **book-architect** or **chapter-builder**, carry forward:
- The book's Tone & Style
- Target Reader profile
- The chapter's stated Purpose and Key Ideas
- Voice specifications

Use these as the editorial benchmark throughout.

## Mode 1 — Diagnostic Edit

Run this before any rewriting when the author wants a full assessment first, or when you need
to understand what kind of editing the chapter actually needs.

### The 8-Lens Audit

Read the chapter through eight lenses. Score each 1–5. Flag specific passages.

```
EDITORIAL DIAGNOSTIC
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

LENS 1 — CLARITY                                    [X / 5]
Are the ideas easy to follow? Are sentences doing one job?
Flag: sentences over 35 words, ambiguous pronouns, buried
      main clause, jargon without definition.

LENS 2 — FLOW & TRANSITIONS                         [X / 5]
Does one idea lead naturally to the next?
Flag: abrupt topic shifts, repeated transition phrases
      ("Furthermore...", "Additionally..."), sections that
      could be reordered without loss.

LENS 3 — AUTHORITY & CONFIDENCE                     [X / 5]
Does the author sound like the expert they are?
Flag: over-hedging ("it could be argued that...", "perhaps",
      "in some ways"), unnecessary qualifiers, passive voice
      hiding the author's stance, apologetic framing.

LENS 4 — FLUFF & PADDING                            [X / 5]
Is every sentence earning its place?
Flag: throat-clearing openers, restated conclusions,
      filler transitions, obvious statements the reader
      already knows, redundant examples.

LENS 5 — SPECIFICITY                                [X / 5]
Are claims backed by concrete detail?
Flag: vague assertions ("many companies do this"),
      unanchored statistics, examples that don't name
      a real person/place/situation.

LENS 6 — VOICE CONSISTENCY                          [X / 5]
Does the chapter sound like one person throughout?
Flag: register shifts (suddenly formal, suddenly casual),
      sentences that could have been written by anyone,
      loss of the author's personality under "professional" pressure.

LENS 7 — STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY                       [X / 5]
Does the chapter arc work? Does every section earn its place?
Flag: sections that repeat each other, a weak opening,
      a close that summarises instead of landing, key ideas
      buried mid-paragraph instead of leading.

LENS 8 — READER EXPERIENCE                          [X / 5]
Would the target reader stay engaged, feel respected,
and come away changed?
Flag: passages that talk down/up to the reader, moments
      where the reader might disengage, promised ideas that
      aren't fully delivered.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
OVERALL EDITORIAL HEALTH:    [X / 40]

TOP 3 PRIORITIES:
1. [Highest-impact fix]
2. [Second priority]
3. [Third priority]

WHAT'S ALREADY WORKING:
[1–3 genuine strengths to preserve — never skip this]
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

After presenting the diagnostic, ask:
> "Want me to go ahead and apply all of these, or focus on the top priorities first?"

## Mode 2 — Deep Edit

A full editorial pass of the entire chapter or section. Apply all fixes simultaneously and
present the revised version alongside an **Edit Log** showing what changed and why.

### The 6 Editorial Passes (apply in this order internally)

Work through these passes silently before presenting the final edit.

#### Pass 1 — Structural Pass
- Reorder sections if the logical flow is wrong
- Split sections doing two jobs into two separate sections
- Delete any section that repeats an earlier one without adding new depth
- Ensure the chapter opens with the strongest hook available and closes with a true payoff

#### Pass 2 — Fluff Removal Pass
Hunt and eliminate without mercy:

| Fluff Pattern | What to do |
|---|---|
| Throat-clearing openers | "In this chapter, we'll explore..." → Delete. Start with the idea. |
| Conclusion restatements | "As we've seen above..." → Cut unless genuinely needed for a long chapter |
| Filler transitions | "Furthermore", "Additionally", "It is worth noting that" → Replace or delete |
| Obvious statements | "This is important." / "As you can imagine." → Cut |
| Redundant pairs | "each and every", "first and foremost", "basic fundamentals" → Keep one word |
| Empty intensifiers | "very", "really", "quite", "rather", "somewhat" → Cut or replace with precision |
| Weak sentence openers | "There is/are...", "It is/was..." → Rewrite to put the subject first |

#### Pass 3 — Clarity Pass
Rewrite for reader ease:

- **Long sentences** (35+ words): break into two or find the main clause and build around it
- **Nested clauses**: untangle — one idea per sentence when ideas are complex
- **Ambiguous pronouns**: replace "it", "this", "they" with the actual noun when unclear
- **Passive voice**: convert to active unless passive is intentional for tone
- **Jargon**: define on first use or replace with plain language
- **Paragraph length**: no paragraph longer than 5 sentences unless it's deliberate list-heavy prose

#### Pass 4 — Authority & Tone Pass
Make the author sound like the expert they are:

**Remove hedges that undermine authority:**
- "could be argued" → "is"
- "it seems that" → delete, or assert the claim directly
- "many people believe" → name who, or make the argument yourself
- "perhaps", "maybe", "might" → cut unless genuine uncertainty is the point
- "I just wanted to say" / "I think what I mean is" → cut entirely

**Replace weak verbs:**
- "is used to" → "does"
- "helps to" → "enables" / "builds" / the precise verb
- "can be seen as" → the direct claim
- "seems to suggest" → "shows" / "proves" / "reveals"

**Strengthen assertions:**
- Vague: "A lot of leaders struggle with this." → Specific: "Most first-time managers spend their first six months avoiding the exact conversations that would solve their problems."
- Passive claim: "It has been found that..." → Active: "Research from [source] shows..." or cut the appeal to authority and just argue it.

#### Pass 5 — Voice Pass
Restore the author's personality where it has been edited out:

- Read 3 sentences aloud. Do they sound like a person or a textbook?
- If the author's brief specified a voice (warm, direct, irreverent) — check every paragraph against it
- Rewrite any sentences that could have been written by anyone
- Add a rhetorical question, an em-dash aside, or a short punchy sentence where the prose has gone flat
- Vary sentence length deliberately: short sentences land punches. Longer ones build momentum and carry the reader through a more complex idea before releasing.

#### Pass 6 — Line Polish Pass
Final surface-level improvements:

- Vary paragraph opening words (flag if 3+ consecutive paragraphs start the same way)
- Fix widows/orphans in reasoning (a point that's introduced but not completed)
- Strengthen the first sentence of every section (it's the most-read sentence in that section)
- Make the last sentence of every section land — it should feel like a small close, not a trailing thought
- Check that the chapter's final paragraph is the strongest version of itself

### Edit Log Format

After presenting the edited chapter, show the Edit Log:

```
─── EDIT LOG ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total edits:    ~[N] changes

STRUCTURAL:     [What was moved, cut, or restructured and why]
FLUFF REMOVED:  [Approx. word reduction + types of cuts made]
CLARITY:        [Key sentence rewrites — before/after 2–3 examples]
AUTHORITY:      [Hedges removed, verbs strengthened — 2–3 examples]
VOICE:          [Where the author's personality was restored]
LINE POLISH:    [Notable surface improvements]

BEFORE word count:   ~[X] words
AFTER word count:    ~[X] words
WORD REDUCTION:      ~[X] words ([N]% leaner)

PRESERVED:      [What was intentionally left unchanged and why]
YOUR CALL:      [1–3 decisions I made that you might want to revisit]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

"Your Call" items are edits where the editor made a judgment call the author may disagree with — flag them explicitly so the author can override.

## Mode 3 — Targeted Fix

Surgical editing of a specific problem. Use this when the author knows exactly what's wrong.

### Targeted Fix Types

#### `"Fix the opening"`
Generate **3 alternative openings** using different hook types. Present all three. Let the author choose.
- Option A: **Scene/Story hook** — drop into a specific moment
- Option B: **Provocation hook** — a bold, slightly uncomfortable claim
- Option C: **Question/Tension hook** — an open loop the reader needs to close

For each option, show the opening paragraph + the bridge that follows.

#### `"Fix the close"`
Generate **2 alternative closings**:
- Option A: **Earned resolution** — the chapter's tension resolves, reader feels satisfied
- Option B: **Forward propulsion** — the close opens a new question or seeds the next chapter

#### `"Remove the fluff from [section/passage]"`
Apply Pass 2 (Fluff Removal) to that section only. Show before/after with word count reduction.

#### `"Make this more authoritative"`
Apply Pass 4 (Authority & Tone) to the passage. List every hedge removed and verb strengthened.

#### `"Improve the flow between [section A] and [section B]"`
Diagnose the gap and rewrite the transition. Offer 2 options:
- A bridge sentence that completes the thought of Section A and opens Section B
- A restructure where the section order is swapped (if that solves the root problem)

#### `"This paragraph feels weak"`
Author pastes a paragraph. Run the 8-lens audit on just that paragraph. Then rewrite it.
Show before/after and name the specific problems fixed.

#### `"Make this punchier"`
Apply rhythm editing: shorten the longest sentences, add a 1-sentence zinger, vary paragraph
length, strengthen verbs. Present the revised version.

#### `"The tone shifts here"`
Identify the exact sentence where the register changes. Rewrite to match the dominant voice of
the chapter.

## Mode 4 — Cross-Chapter Review

Used when the author has completed 2+ chapters and wants to review consistency, arc coherence,
and whether the book as a whole is working.

### What to review

**Voice Consistency Across Chapters**
- Does the author sound the same in Chapter 1 and Chapter 5?
- Flag: chapters where a different tone has crept in (usually because they were written at different times)
- Fix: identify the 3 defining voice characteristics from the strongest chapter; apply as the standard

**Conceptual Consistency**
- Are key terms defined the same way throughout?
- Does the book's central framework appear consistently or shift in subtle ways?
- Flag: the same concept called different things in different chapters

**Pacing & Arc**
- Do early chapters feel too slow or too dense for a reader new to the topic?
- Does the book build in complexity at the right rate?
- Flag: a chapter that front-loads too much, or a chapter that feels like a repeat
- Suggest: reordering chapters if the arc isn't building

**Chapter Openings & Closings**
- Do all 12 (or N) chapters open with the same type of hook? (If so, vary them)
- Do chapters end with momentum or do they deflate?
- Does each chapter's close set up the next?

**Promises Kept**
- Scan for claims made in early chapters ("in Chapter 7, we'll cover...") and verify they're kept
- Flag: any unfulfilled promise or forward reference that leads nowhere

**Repetition Audit**
- Flag: stories, statistics, or examples used more than once
- Flag: ideas that appear in multiple chapters without clearly building on each other

### Cross-Chapter Report Format

```
CROSS-CHAPTER EDITORIAL REPORT
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CHAPTERS REVIEWED:   [List]

VOICE CONSISTENCY:   [Rating + key observations]
CONCEPTUAL DRIFT:    [Any terms/frameworks used inconsistently]
PACING:              [Slow/fast chapters flagged, overall arc assessment]
CHAPTER OPENINGS:    [Variety assessment + weakest opener flagged]
CHAPTER CLOSINGS:    [Energy level at chapter ends + best/weakest]
UNFULFILLED PROMISES:[Any forward references not delivered]
REPETITION:          [Stories/stats/ideas used more than once]

TOP 5 CROSS-CHAPTER FIXES:
1.
2.
3.
4.
5.

WHAT'S WORKING WELL ACROSS THE BOOK:
[Genuine strengths of the manuscript as a whole]
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## Editorial Principles

These guide every edit, regardless of mode.

**1. Preserve the author's voice above all else.**
The goal is never to make it sound like a professional editor wrote it. The goal is to make it
sound like the best version of the author. If an edit would make the writing technically correct
but personality-less, don't make it.

**2. Cut, don't dilute.**
When something is weak, the instinct is to add a caveat. The correct move is usually to cut
the weak part entirely. A chapter of 2,800 sharp words beats 3,500 padded ones every time.

**3. Earn every adjective.**
"Great", "powerful", "transformative" are the most overused words in non-fiction. Replace with
the specific thing that makes it great/powerful/transformative.

**4. The reader is smart.**
Never explain what you just said. Trust the reader to follow. Repetition for *emphasis* is
different from repetition from *insecurity about whether the reader got it*.

**5. Show the author what you changed and why.**
Every edit is a teaching moment. The Edit Log isn't just documentation — it's coaching. An
author who understands why a hedge was removed writes without hedges next time.

**6. Flag, don't fix, when the problem is substance.**
If a section is weak because the *idea* is underdeveloped (not just the prose), flag it and
send it back to the author. The editor's job is not to invent the author's ideas for them.

## Integration with Book Architect & Chapter Builder

This skill sits at the end of the pipeline:

```
book-architect  →  chapter-builder  →  editor-sharpener
  (Plan)              (Draft)              (Polish)
```

**From book-architect**, carry forward as the editorial benchmark:
- Tone & Style specification
- Target Reader profile
- The book's Core Premise and Reader Promise

**From chapter-builder**, use as chapter context:
- The Chapter Brief (Purpose, Key Ideas, Intended Close)
- The Craft Note (what the drafter flagged as thin or assumed)
- Story spine and section structure

The **Craft Note** from chapter-builder is particularly valuable — it pre-identifies where the
chapter is weakest. Start the editorial pass there.

After completing an edit, offer:
> "Want me to move to the next chapter, or run a cross-chapter review once a few more are done?"

Keep the same **Chapter Status** tracker used in chapter-builder, adding an edit column:

```
CHAPTER STATUS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Ch 1: [Title] ........... ✅ Draft  ✅ Edited
Ch 2: [Title] ........... ✅ Draft  ✅ Edited
Ch 3: [Title] ........... ✅ Draft  ✍️  Editing
Ch 4: [Title] ........... ✍️  Drafting
Ch 5: [Title] ........... ⬜ Not started
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```
